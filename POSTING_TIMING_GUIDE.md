# 🕐 Posting Timing Guide — Instagram Reels & YouTube Shorts

*Researched 19 Nov 2026. Three answers in one place: **India (IST)**, **Global (local-time)**, and **Whole-world overlap (UTC)**.*

---

## 0. TL;DR — the cheat sheet

| Audience | Instagram Reels | YouTube Shorts |
|---|---|---|
| 🇮🇳 **India** (Hindi / Indian audience) | **Tue–Thu, 7:00–9:00 PM IST** (upload ~6:30–7:00 PM) · backup 12:00–1:30 PM · weekend Sat 11 AM–1 PM, Sun 8–10 PM | **Evenings 7:00–10:00 PM IST** (upload ~5:00–6:30 PM so the test phase runs into peak) · best days lean Thu–Sun |
| 🌍 **Global** (English, US/UK-centric, *local* time) | **Wed & Thu** · 6–10 PM local · plus Wed 12 PM, Thu 7–9 AM | **Friday 4–7 PM local** · Sat 11 AM–3 PM · Sun 8–11 AM · Mon 10 AM–1 PM |
| 🌐 **Whole-world overlap** (mixed audience, one upload) | **14:00–15:30 UTC** (= 7:30–9 PM IST) → best single slot · **17:00–19:00 UTC** as the second | Same two UTC slots; add **Fri 21:00–23:00 UTC** for the US/Europe weekend push |

**Three rules that survive every dataset:**
1. **Evenings on weekdays beat everything else for Reels**, and **midweek (Tue–Thu)** is the most-repeated best-day finding.
2. **Never post 1–5 AM in your audience's local time.** Every study agrees. (PostFast measured a Tuesday 01:00 UTC slot at a median reach of ~18 accounts vs. ~2,000 at the best slot.)
3. **Timing is a tie-breaker, not a growth lever.** Hook, retention and consistency matter more (see §6).

## 1. How much to trust this (read first)

There is **no single true "best time"**. The public studies disagree, and I'd rather show that than hide it.

| Study | Sample | What it says | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Buffer — Instagram](https://buffer.com/resources/when-is-the-best-time-to-post-on-instagram/) | 9.6M posts, Jan 2024–Dec 2025 | Best: **Thu 9 AM, Wed 12 PM, Wed 6 PM**; evenings 6–11 PM lead; worst 1–5 AM, Fri/Sat | **All formats pooled** — not Reels-only. Metric is engagement rate, not reach |
| [Later — Reels](https://later.com/blog/best-time-to-post-on-instagram/) | ~975K Reels | **Midnight**, Monday best; 12–6 AM window strong | Contradicts Buffer. Likely an engagement-*rate* artifact (low-reach hours = small, loyal audience) |
| [PostFast — Reels](https://postfa.st/blog/best-time-to-post-reels-on-instagram) | 5,191 Reels, 90 days | Weekday **17:00–19:00 UTC**; Sat ~14:00 UTC (10 AM ET) top multiplier | Vendor data, small cells, no significance testing. Big accounts skew raw reach |
| [PostEverywhere — Reels](https://posteverywhere.ai/blog/best-time-to-schedule-instagram-reels) | 8,500+ Reels | Sun 1–4 PM, Thu 11 AM–2 PM, Fri best day | Vendor data |
| [Buffer — YouTube](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-youtube/) | ~1.8M videos | **Shorts: Fri 4 / 6 / 7 PM**; Fri > Sat > Thu; Mon & Tue weakest | No per-slot sample sizes. Other write-ups of the same data quote Wed 4 PM |
| [PostEverywhere — Shorts](https://posteverywhere.ai/blog/best-time-to-schedule-youtube-shorts) | 6,500+ Shorts, Dec 2025–Oct 2026 | **Fri 5 PM (+42%)**, Sun 9 AM (+35%), Sat 3 PM (+32%), Mon noon (+30%). **6–9 PM was −8%** (crowded). Thursday weakest | Every *day* is within ±11% of normal — timing effect is small |
| India blogs ([MyDigitalSevak](https://mydigitalsevak.in/blog/best-time-to-post-instagram-india/), [InfluencerMetric](https://influencermetric.in/blog/best-time-to-post-india), others) | Mostly no stated method | **7–9 PM IST weekdays**; 12–2 PM backup | SEO blogs. Consistent with each other, but not hard data |

**Important honesty notes**
- **There is no rigorous India-only dataset I could find** for either platform. The India section below is *India blog consensus + global studies' local-time patterns*, not measured India data.
- The big studies (Buffer, Later) **normalise to each viewer's local time**. So "6–10 PM" means 6–10 PM *where your viewer is* — this is why the world section (§4) needs time-zone arithmetic.
- Several "2026 data" blog claims trace back to 2024 data. Treat all numbers as **starting hypotheses to test**, not rules.
- Instagram's often-quoted "first hour is critical" is a creator heuristic, not a confirmed Instagram rule. Meta's stated top Reels signals are watch time (completion), likes-per-reach and DM shares.

## 2. 🇮🇳 India — IST (UTC+5:30, no daylight saving)

### 2.1 Why evenings
Indian audiences are heavily mobile-first (EY: ~5 hrs/day on phones; InMobi: ~4 hrs 5 min, with a **6:30–9:30 AM** morning-video habit and **higher weekend** viewing). The evening pattern (after work/college, commute, dinner) is the consistent blog finding.

### 2.2 Instagram Reels — India week plan

| Day | Primary | Backup | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | 7:00–9:00 PM | 12:00–1:00 PM | Start-of-week scroll |
| **Tue** | **7:00–9:00 PM** ⭐ | 11 AM–1 PM | Peak weekday |
| **Wed** | **7:00–9:00 PM** ⭐ | 12:00–2:00 PM | Peak weekday (also top day in Buffer's global data) |
| **Thu** | **7:00–9:00 PM** ⭐ | 12:00–1:00 PM | Global data also shows a Thu 7–9 AM spike |
| Fri | 7:00–9:00 PM | 12:00–1:00 PM | Competition from going-out/weekend plans — test it |
| Sat | 11:00 AM–1:00 PM | 8:00–10:00 PM | Late-morning leisure scroll |
| Sun | 8:00–10:00 PM ⭐ | 11 AM–1 PM | Strong "last evening of the weekend" slot |

- **Upload ~30–60 min before the window** so the first test audience arrives as traffic ramps. (Heuristic; not proven.)
- **Stories** (not Reels): 7–9 AM and 8–10 PM.
- **Niche shifts:** cafés/fashion/lifestyle 8–10 PM · food 11 AM–12:30 PM & 6:30–8 PM (before meals) · B2B/edtech 12–2 PM · weddings/jewellery weekend late mornings.

### 2.3 YouTube Shorts — India
- **Peak viewing 7:00–10:00 PM IST**; weekends skew to afternoon + evening.
- Shorts are pushed to **strangers via the feed** and keep being re-tested for days, so upload time matters *less* than for Reels. The practical move: **upload 5:00–6:30 PM IST** so the first viewer test happens as evening traffic builds.
- Global Shorts data points to **Fri–Sun** as strongest (Buffer: Fri > Sat > Thu). For India, **Thu/Fri/Sat 7–9 PM** and **Sun 6–9 PM** are the days to try first.
- Shorts have a long tail (creator reports of views months later; not rigorously measured), so don't panic over a slow first day.

### 2.4 Avoid (India)
- **1:00–5:00 AM** (all sources).
- **Mid-afternoon 2–5 PM on weekdays** (work/college hours; Buffer's weekday afternoons are weakest outside Friday).
- **Live-event clashes** — *my inference, not from the studies:* an IPL/India match evening, big festival rush hours, or a viral TV event can pull attention away. Check the fixture list before launching a major story.

Times are **the viewer's local time**.

| Best day | Best local windows |
|---|---|
| **Wed** (best) | 12 PM · 6 PM · 8 AM |
| **Thu** | **7–9 AM (peak 9 AM)** — the one weekday where morning beats evening |
| Tue | 7 PM · 3 PM · 5 PM |
| Mon | 6–8 PM |
| Fri | 9–10 PM |
| Sat | 9–10 PM (also PostFast: ~10 AM ET) |
| Sun | 8–10 PM (also PostEverywhere: 1–4 PM) |

*Source: [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/when-is-the-best-time-to-post-on-instagram/), cross-checked with PostFast and PostEverywhere.*

**Contested:** Later/Dash Social say midnight–6 AM. I'd **not** build a schedule on it (engagement-rate artifact — see §1), but it's a cheap test for a niche loyal audience.

### 3.2 YouTube Shorts
| Rank | Slot (local) | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Fri 4–7 PM** | Buffer top-3 slots; PostEverywhere Fri 5 PM (+42%) — **two independent studies agree** |

| ⚠️ | 6–9 PM on busy days | PostEverywhere: crowded, −8% vs. channel norm |

## 4. 🌐 Whole-world overlap — one upload, many time zones

> **This section is my synthesis**: the studies' *local-time* findings + time-zone arithmetic. It is **not** a measured dataset. Use it to pick slots, then test.

### 4.1 The logic
Local-time peaks cluster around **late morning/lunch (11 AM–2 PM)** and **evening (6–10 PM)**. Since one video is posted once, the best shared slot is where **India's evening lands on Europe's afternoon, the Gulf's evening and the US East's morning.**

### 4.2 The two golden UTC windows

| | UTC | India (IST) | UAE | UK | C. Europe | US East | US West | Brazil | Singapore |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **🥇 G1** | **14:00–15:30** | **7:30–9:00 PM** | 6:00–7:30 PM | 2:00–3:30 PM | 3:00–4:30 PM | 9:00–10:30 AM | 6:00–7:30 AM | 11 AM–12:30 PM | 10:00–11:30 PM |
| **🥈 G2** | **17:00–19:00** | 10:30 PM–12:30 AM | 9:00–11:00 PM | 5:00–7:00 PM | 6:00–8:00 PM | 12:00–2:00 PM | 9:00–11:00 AM | 2:00–4:00 PM | 1:00–3:00 AM |

*Table valid for northern-hemisphere winter (now → mid-March). In summer, UK/Europe/US clocks read **+1 hour** for the same UTC; India, UAE, Singapore and Brazil don't change.*

**Why these two**
- **G1** hits the India evening peak, *and* Buffer's Thursday 9 AM top slot (US East), *and* PostFast's Saturday 14:00 UTC top multiplier. Best if India is your largest audience.
- **G2** matches PostFast's strongest weekday window (17:00–19:00 UTC) — US lunch + UK/EU after-work. Best if your audience is West-heavy; India sees it late-night (still active).
- **Skip for a world upload:** 21:00–02:00 UTC (US evening) **kills India reach** (2:30–7:30 AM IST). Use it only for a US-targeted video.

### 4.3 Add-on: Friday Shorts push
Friday 4–7 PM in the US/Europe is the strongest Shorts finding. 4 PM ET = **21:00 UTC (winter) = 2:30 AM IST Saturday**. Schedule it ahead via YouTube Studio so an India-based creator doesn't need to be awake.

### 4.4 Worst, everywhere
**1–5 AM local**, and for Reels **Friday/Saturday daytime** in Buffer's data.

## 5. 🎯 What to use for *this* studio

| Content | Language/audience | Slot |
|---|---|---|
| **Kahani Stories** (Hindi mythology, e.g. *Karna Part 1*) | India-first | Reels: **Tue/Wed/Thu 7:00–9:00 PM IST**, plus **Sun 8–10 PM IST**. Shorts: **Thu–Sun 7–9 PM IST** |
| **English global shorts** (viral explainers/stories) | World | **G1 (14:00–15:30 UTC)** Tue–Thu · G2 as backup · Friday Shorts push at 4–7 PM US time |
| **Series (Part 1 → Part 2)** | India | Same weekday & same time every week — habit builds the early audience your retention signal needs |

**Cross-posting plan (same video, both platforms):**
1. Post the **Reel at the golden slot**.
2. Post the **Short ~30–60 min later** (or, if the Short is the same file, on a different evening that week) so the two don't compete for the same viewers' attention.
3. Use the scheduler: Instagram (Meta Business Suite / app) and YouTube Studio both schedule natively.

## 6. 🧪 Verify it yourself (90 days)

The studies' own conclusion: *"generic charts are a decent starting point and a terrible finishing point."*

**Where to read your real audience clock**
- **Instagram:** Insights → **Total followers → Most active times** (hours + days, in your timezone).
- **YouTube:** Studio → Analytics → **Audience → "When your viewers are on YouTube"** (last 28 days).

**Test design**
1. Pick **3 candidate slots** (e.g. Tue 7 PM IST, Thu 12:30 PM IST, Sun 9 PM IST).
2. Post **4–5 videos per slot** before judging (single posts are noise).
3. Compare **views/reach at 72 h ÷ your channel median** — not raw numbers.
4. Keep content type constant across slots; change *only* the time.
5. Keep the winning slot for **2–3 weeks** before moving it.

**Log template**

## 7. What matters more than the clock

- **Hook & retention** — Reels are ranked on completion and DM shares; Shorts on viewer-vs-swiped-away. PostFast: *three well-timed Reels a week beat one perfectly timed Reel.*
- **Consistency** — same slot weekly beats clever timing once.
- **Shorts have a long tail** — a poor slot rarely sinks a Short permanently; a poor hook does.
- **Post when you can reply** — *my suggestion, not a studied finding:* answering early comments is cheap and may help early engagement.
